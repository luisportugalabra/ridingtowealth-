The Three Strategies

How each strategy works

Strategy 1

Value Composite — 18.61% CAGR

Rank every stock in the market using six fundamental metrics: P/E, P/S, P/B, P/CF, EV/EBITDA, and Shareholder Yield. Combine them into a single composite score (VC2). Buy the 25 cheapest. Rebalance annually.

Financial stocks are excluded because P/B and EV/EBITDA are not meaningful for banks and insurers. Stocks with negative earnings are also removed.

Historically, this strategy performs best when including small caps — market cap above $200M is the sweet spot that balances liquidity with the small-cap value premium.

Strategy 2

Trending Value — 21.19% CAGR

Start with the Value strategy. Identify the cheapest 10% of stocks by composite score. Then, within that cheap decile, rank by 6-month price momentum and buy the top 25.

This combines two of the strongest factors in finance — value and momentum — into one strategy. You’re buying cheap stocks that are already moving up, avoiding value traps.

Like the Value strategy, this works best with $200M+ market cap, and excludes financials and negative earnings.

Strategy 3

Intermediate Momentum — 21.7% CAGR

Based on Novy-Marx (2012) academic research. Instead of looking at recent returns (which tend to reverse), this strategy uses returns from months 7–12 — the “intermediate” momentum window that has been shown to persist.

Calculate: 12-month return minus 6-month return. Buy the top 25 stocks. Rebalance annually.

Unlike value strategies, momentum historically performs better on larger, more liquid stocks. $2B+ market cap tends to deliver the best risk-adjusted returns, closely matching the S&P 500 universe where the original research was conducted.

How I invest

Why I run all three

Running multiple strategies lowers overall portfolio variance and improves the Sharpe ratio. Value and momentum are negatively correlated — when one underperforms, the other tends to pick up the slack. Combining them smooths out returns and makes the portfolio easier to hold through drawdowns.

That’s why systematic strategies are powerful: you don’t need to think, analyse, or second-guess. The rules run themselves. I split my capital 50% in my active picks, 50% across these three strategies. The systematic half requires almost zero time — just one rebalance per year.
