The Thesis

Why Intermediate Momentum Works

The Intermediate Momentum strategy is based on research by Robert Novy-Marx (2012), building on work by Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) and Moskowitz & Pedersen (2011). The core insight: momentum measured over months 7–12 of the past year is far more predictive than short-term (last 3 months) or long-term (5+ years) momentum.

“Recent returns tend to reverse. Very old returns tend to mean-revert. But intermediate returns — the sweet spot of 7 to 12 months — persist. This is momentum at its most powerful.”

How It Works

The strategy is brutally simple:

1. Take the S&P 500 universe
2. Rank all stocks by their return over months 7–12 (ignoring the most recent 6 months)
3. Buy the top 25 stocks
4. Equal weight at 4% each
5. Rebalance annually in January
6. Repeat

By skipping the most recent 6 months, the strategy avoids short-term reversal — the tendency for recent winners to snap back. Instead, it captures “validated” momentum: stocks that gained significantly 7–12 months ago and are likely to continue trending.

Why Skip the Last 6 Months?

Short-term returns (1–6 months) contain a lot of noise: earnings surprises, news reactions, short squeezes. These often reverse. But returns from 7–12 months ago reflect structural shifts — improving fundamentals, sector rotation, institutional accumulation — that tend to persist.

The Evidence

Momentum Is One of the Strongest Factors in Finance

Momentum is not a fluke. It is one of the most documented anomalies in financial markets, observed across decades, geographies, and asset classes:

Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) — First documented that stocks with high past returns continue to outperform over 3–12 month horizons.

Moskowitz & Pedersen (2011) — Showed momentum works across equities, bonds, currencies, and commodities. This is not a stock-specific anomaly.

Novy-Marx (2012) — Proved that intermediate momentum (months 7–12) is significantly more powerful than recent momentum, and less prone to crashes.

“Momentum is everywhere and it is strong. The intermediate horizon is where it is most reliable — far enough from noise, close enough to capture the trend.”

The behavioral explanation is straightforward: investors underreact to good news. Information takes time to be fully reflected in prices. By the time the market catches up, intermediate momentum has already captured the move.

Risk Assessment

What Can Go Wrong

High
Momentum Crashes
Momentum strategies can suffer sharp drawdowns during market reversals (e.g., 2008: -44%). When markets crash and recover, last year's losers become winners and momentum gets punished.
Medium
Higher Volatility
26.1% annual volatility vs 17.4% for the S&P 500. The ride is bumpier, even if the destination is better. Not suitable for investors who can't handle drawdowns.
Medium
Concentrated Portfolio
Only 25 stocks. A few bad picks can significantly impact annual returns. Diversification is lower than a broad index.
Low
Transaction Costs
Annual rebalance means relatively low turnover. With modern brokers charging zero commissions, this is a minor concern for most investors.
Conclusion

Momentum Keeps Very Strong

Over 20 years, intermediate momentum turned $1 into $50.51 vs $7.95 for the S&P 500. That is 6.3x the return of passive investing. The Sharpe ratio of 0.95 confirms this isn't just about taking more risk — the risk-adjusted returns are genuinely superior.

The strategy is simple, rules-based, and backed by decades of academic research. It requires no stock-picking skill, no market timing, and no discretionary judgment. Just follow the rules and rebalance once a year.
