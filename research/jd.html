Investment Thesis

The Market is Pricing Permanent Damage to a Temporary Problem

JD.com trades at ~7.6x forward earnings — a discount of roughly 60% to its Chinese e-commerce peers and 70% to global comparables. The implied valuation, net of US$29B in cash, ascribes essentially zero value to the world's most sophisticated retail logistics network, a dominant position in electronics and appliances, and a portfolio of high-growth adjacent businesses.

The market's concern is legitimate but misdirected: JD launched JD Food Delivery in 2024, triggering a period of heavy investment that compressed group-level margins and obscured the steady improvement in the core retail business. The market has conflated cyclical investment losses with structural deterioration.

JD Retail's operating margin expanded from 4.0% in FY2024 to 4.6% in FY2025 — a full-year record — even as the company simultaneously funded the fastest-scaling food delivery launch in China's history.

The core thesis is simple: if JD Retail stabilises at US$8B+ in operating income (already achieved), the New Businesses investment cycle peaks in 2026–2027, and the market re-rates the stock to a still-conservative 10–12x multiple, the implied return over 3 years is 2–3.5x from current prices. Cash provides a hard floor.

JD Retail — The Engine

The core business is a best-in-class supply chain operation with 90%+ of orders fulfilled same-day or next-day. Operating margins expanded 60bps in FY2025 to 4.6%, a record, driven by rising ad revenue mix and improved category economics. The path to 6%+ margins is structural: advertising penetration is still below Alibaba's comparable segment, and JD's first-party inventory model becomes more valuable as brands seek channel integrity. We model 5.5–6.0% retail margins by 2028.

JD Logistics — Undervalued Infrastructure

JD Logistics is listed separately (HKEX: 2618) and is one of the few end-to-end supply chain operators in China with cold-chain, bulk, and last-mile capabilities under one roof. The external revenue business (selling logistics to third parties) is growing at 20%+ and is not reflected in the parent's valuation. We treat this segment as optionality — any re-rating of the listed subsidiary benefits JD.com proportionally.

New Businesses — The Overhang, and the Opportunity

JD Food Delivery launched in 2024 and scaled to meaningful GMV by Q4 2025, with sequential loss narrowing every quarter since launch. This is the swing factor in the investment case. The market is pricing a permanently loss-making segment. The base case is that losses peak in mid-2026 as JD gains density in core cities and subsidy intensity rationalises. JD's structural advantage is its existing logistics network, which gives it an inherent cost advantage in unit economics once density is achieved.

Catalyst Timeline

What Closes the Gap

H2 2026
Food delivery losses peak and begin sequential narrowing
Management guided Q4 2025 as the peak investment quarter. If H1 2026 results confirm narrowing losses, the single largest overhang on the stock begins to lift. This is the near-term catalyst.
2026–27
Buyback programme accelerates at distressed prices
US$5B programme (Aug 2024) with ~US$2B remaining as of March 2026. At US$30, each dollar of buyback retires ~3.7% more NAV than the stock implies — highly accretive. Expect programme renewal.
2027
New Businesses approaching breakeven
Every quarter of sequential loss narrowing in food delivery removes drag from group earnings. At breakeven, group Non-GAAP EBITDA jumps from US$2.6B to US$7B+ without any retail growth — a multiple re-rating event.
2027–28
JD Retail margin reaches 5.5%+ — visible to consensus
Sell-side upgrades typically happen 2–4 quarters after a new margin regime is established. When 5.5%+ becomes the consensus expectation, the forward P/E re-rating follows. Target: 10–12x.
Optionality
Subsidiary spin-offs or listings
JD Health (HK-listed), JD Industrials, and JD Property are embedded in the retail segment. Any formal separation unlocks sum-of-parts value that the market is currently ignoring entirely.
Risk Assessment

What Could Go Wrong

High
Capital misallocation
US$29B cash is the thesis's foundation — and its biggest risk. A large, ill-conceived acquisition could destroy years of value creation. This is the #1 risk to monitor.
High
PDD erosion of core e-commerce
Pinduoduo has taken meaningful share in mass-market electronics. If this extends upmarket into JD's premium positioning, revenue growth slows and the margin expansion thesis stalls.
Medium
Food delivery war of attrition
Meituan and Douyin have deeper pockets and stronger network effects. If the food delivery loss-narrowing trajectory reverses, the New Businesses overhang becomes permanent.
Medium
China macro / consumer weakness
JD is exposed to Chinese consumer spending. Prolonged weakness in electronics and home appliance demand compresses both revenue growth and operating leverage.
Medium
Regulatory risk
China's regulatory posture toward large internet platforms has moderated since 2021–22 but has not disappeared. Any renewed focus could weigh on the stock.
Low
VIE structure / geopolitical
Applicable to US-listed ADRs. For investors holding via HKEX (9618), this risk is substantially mitigated. Recommend HK listing over ADR for long-term holders.
Conclusion

A Margin-of-Safety Investment in a World-Class Business

At US$29.75, the market is offering JD.com's core retail operation — one of the most capable supply chain businesses in the world — at 5x operating income, net of a cash pile that represents 76% of the current market cap. This is the kind of mispricing that occurs when a short-term investment cycle (food delivery) is confused with a structural deterioration of the business.

The bear case is protected by cash. The base case requires only that JD continues doing what it has been doing — expanding retail margins and narrowing food delivery losses — and that the market eventually notices. The bull case does not require extraordinary execution; it requires JD to reach the profitability levels that its own cost structure implies are achievable.

At 7.6x earnings on a business generating US$7.4B in retail operating income, with US$29B in net cash, and the most capable logistics infrastructure in China — the risk is not that you're wrong. The risk is that you're right too early.

For a 3-year holder without tax friction, the expected value of this position is strongly positive across all reasonable scenarios. The position size should reflect the genuine uncertainty around the food delivery investment cycle, but the asymmetry — hard floor from cash, 2–4x upside in base and bull cases — is among the most compelling in China tech today.
